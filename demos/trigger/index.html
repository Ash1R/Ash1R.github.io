Talk to your phone without "Hey Google"

It should respond to requests spoken to the phone, and ignore a video playing, people across the room, and noise. Try: hold the phone ~40 cm away and ask "what's the capital of Japan?". Then play a talking video near it, or talk from across the room. Volume up, silent switch off. Everything runs on the phone; no audio leaves it.

How it works

Every sound goes through a cascade. Each stage only runs if the previous one passed, so the expensive models stay off most of the time:

  1. Speech? Loudness and pitch periodicity, every 10 ms (hand-written DSP).
  2. Close to the phone? How far the voice rises above the room's noise floor. The threshold (27.5 dB) was set on round 1 of my phone recordings. On round 2 it rejected all far talk, video and noise (0 of 14), and passed 10 of 10 utterances spoken to the phone.
  3. What was said? Whisper-tiny (pretrained, in-browser).
  4. Is it a request? Nearest examples in a pretrained sentence-embedding space (MiniLM). This is the weak stage. It still lets through some chat said close to the phone, and fixing that needs real data.

The orange trace is the research part. The phone plays an inaudible 19 kHz tone, and lip/jaw motion Doppler-shifts its echo. A self-supervised model I trained on that echo matched audio to the right echo moment 60% of the time when I talked to the phone, against 19% for a video (chance 12%). On my 4 minutes of labeled data it didn't yet beat the loudness cue, so it's shown here but not used in the decision.
